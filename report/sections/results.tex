\subsection{Channel Reproduction Results}
\label{sec:channel-results}

We summarize the six official Week~2 runs of the two channel cases
under Go 1.10.8 on Linux/amd64. Each case was executed in three separate
test invocations. Define the outcome indicator as 1 when a run exhibits
the expected symptom and 0 otherwise. Both cases matched their expected
outcome in all three runs (3/3), giving an indicator mean of 1.00 and a
sample standard deviation of 0.00.

\paragraph{Kubernetes \#5316 (Figure~1).}
In all three runs, the test timed out after five seconds, and the worker
stack showed \texttt{[chan send]}. The parent stops receiving after its
timeout. The worker then attempts to send on an unbuffered channel,
which requires a matching receiver, so it cannot complete.
Our model deliberately orders these events and adds a test-only wait
to keep the blocked worker observable. The resulting test timeout is
not a report from Go's runtime deadlock detector.

\paragraph{Docker \#24007 (Figure~10).}
All three runs terminated with an unrecovered
\texttt{close of closed channel} panic. Both workers are forced to
enter the \texttt{default} branch before either closes the channel.
Checking whether a channel is closed and then closing it are not one
atomic operation: after the first close succeeds, the second close
panics. This is a non-blocking message-passing bug in the paper's
classification~\cite{tu2019go}.

\paragraph{Implication for Claim~C1.}
The Kubernetes case illustrates how message passing can leave a
goroutine blocked when the receiver abandons the communication.
Channels alone do not ensure that sender and receiver lifetimes are
coordinated. The Docker case illustrates a separate channel lifecycle
error. These selected models provide mechanism-level evidence, but
cannot estimate the share of blocking bugs caused by message passing.
The observed 3/3 outcomes reflect controlled executions rather than
natural triggering probabilities in the original applications.

\subsection{Shared-Memory Reproduction Results}
\label{sec:shared-memory-results}

We ran both the buggy and fixed variants of Docker \#27037 and
Docker \#25384 three times with \texttt{go test -race} under
Go 1.10.8 on Linux/amd64.

\paragraph{Docker \#27037 (Figure 8 of Tu et al.~\cite{tu2019go}).}
The race detector reported a data race in all three buggy runs.
In the first run, the report showed a worker goroutine reading the
loop variable \texttt{i} at line~19 of \texttt{docker\_race.go}
and the parent goroutine writing to the same address at line~14.
The fixed variant passed all three runs with no race warnings.

\paragraph{Docker \#25384 (Figure 5 of Tu et al.~\cite{tu2019go}).}
All three buggy runs timed out after five seconds. In the first run,
task~0 finished, but the stack trace showed the test still blocked
in \texttt{sync.(*WaitGroup).Wait}. Calling \texttt{Wait()} inside
the loop prevented the second task from starting. After moving
\texttt{Wait()} outside the loop, the fixed variant passed all
three runs. Neither variant emitted a race warning.

Figure~\ref{fig:reproduction-outcomes} summarizes the expected outcomes,
and the right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:race-detection} shows the number
of runs with race warnings for each variant.
